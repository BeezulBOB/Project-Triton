# Project Triton RDT

# PT-SA-000 Engineering Principles

**Document ID:** PT-SA-000

**Revision:** 0.1

**Status:** Draft

---

# 1 Purpose

This document establishes the permanent engineering principles governing Project Triton.

These principles define how engineering decisions shall be made throughout the project.

Whenever two competing designs appear technically viable, the preferred solution should be the one that better satisfies these principles.

---

# 2 Principle 1 – Open Center

The thruster shall maintain a continuous unobstructed flow passage through its center.

No shaft, hub, stationary support, bearing, wiring, grille, or structural member shall cross the central opening.

This principle has priority over convenience of manufacture.

---

# 3 Principle 2 – Modularity

Every major subsystem should be independently replaceable.

Examples include:

- Blade cartridge
- Rotor
- Magnet carrier
- Stator
- Bearing cartridge
- Housing
- Duct

Replacing one module should not require redesigning unrelated modules.

---

# 4 Principle 3 – Parametric Design

Important geometry shall be controlled by Fusion 360 User Parameters.

Dimensions shall not be duplicated unnecessarily throughout sketches.

The design shall scale through parameter modification rather than manual remodeling whenever practical.

---

# 5 Principle 4 – Traceability

Important engineering decisions shall be documented.

Design changes shall include technical justification.

Experimental results shall be retained.

Engineering history shall never depend upon memory.

---

# 6 Principle 5 – Test Before Optimization

The project shall first demonstrate functionality.

Optimization shall only occur after the underlying mechanism has been experimentally validated.

Performance improvements shall be supported by measured data whenever practical.

---

# 7 Principle 6 – Serviceability

Wear components should be removable.

Inspection should not require destructive disassembly.

Fasteners should remain accessible.

Maintenance should require ordinary tools whenever practical.

---

# 8 Principle 7 – Simplicity

When two solutions provide comparable performance, the simpler solution shall normally be preferred.

Complexity must provide measurable value.

---

# 9 Principle 8 – Manufacturability

Prototype components should favor manufacturing methods available to an advanced hobbyist or small laboratory.

Preferred methods include:

- FDM printing
- Resin printing
- Basic CNC machining
- Manual machining

---

# 10 Principle 9 – Measurability

Engineering decisions should be validated through measurable quantities including:

- Thrust
- Voltage
- Current
- RPM
- Temperature
- Efficiency
- Vibration
- Noise

Assumptions should eventually be replaced by experimental data.

---

# 11 Principle 10 – Controlled Evolution

Project Triton shall evolve through documented revisions.

Each prototype should answer specific engineering questions.

Future designs should build upon measured results rather than replacing previous work without analysis.
